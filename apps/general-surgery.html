How top students answer a viva
  1. Pause 3 seconds. Listen for every part of the question: "what do you look for, and what confirms it" is two answers.
  2. Your first sentence is the answer. No background, no repeating the question.
  3. Then follow one route. The question decides what comes first. Name the groups, not a number ("pre-renal, renal, post-renal", never "there are 8").
  4. Most important and most common first. Say why in a few words, and name the danger you must not miss. An unstable patient: safety first.
  5. Stop. Let the examiner ask the next question. Keep the follow-up ready.
  6. Stuck? Start from what you know (the definition, the groups, the mechanism) and reason aloud. Never invent. "Are you sure?" means think again, not argue.
